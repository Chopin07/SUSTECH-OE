3.1 Light & Matter Interaction
3
The physical basis of laser: The resonant interaction betweenelectromagnetic fields and the (bound) electron inside atoms (or ions ,molecules) which form the matter.Laser theory: Light & Matter1) The Classical Theory– Classical atomic luminous modelDescription of light: classical electromagnetic field (Maxwellequations)Description of atom: classical atomic model (dipole resonator)You can approximate describe absorption, dispersion, spontaneous emission andspontaneous emission line-width and other physical phenomena, can notdescribe the nonlinear physical process (saturated, nonlinear polarization, etc.)

--- page 4 ---
4
2) Semi-Classical Theory– the Lamb theory (W. E. Lamb Jr, 1964)Description of light: classical electromagnetic field (Maxwell equations)Description of atom: quantum mechanicsCan deal with the volatility of light and the relevant physical phenomena likenon-linear phenomena and Lamb dip, etc., but can not deal with the related issuesof the particle nature of light (quantum optics), such as the quantum fluctuationsof light, and the photon statistics.3) Quantum Theory– quantum electrodynamics (QED) usingquantum processing to deal with radiation field and atomDescription of light: photons by using QEDDescription of atom: QED modelIt is the foundation of modern quantum optics, the physical problems associatedwith particles of light can be handled, but very complicate in handling issuesrelated to the volatility of light (such as phase). In QED, the number of photons(i.e. light amplitude) and the phase is a quantity of uncertainty.

Characteristics of the saturation of population inversion
7
1. The saturation intensity has minimum value at central frequency, which is equal toIs=hv0/σ21τ2. When the frequency of the incident light is far away from the centralfrequency, the corresponding saturation intensity will increase.2. For specific incident light intensity: the smaller saturation intensity, the quickerdecrease of Δn, and therefore the more serious saturation effect.3. The saturation effect is most pronounced when the incident light frequencymatches the central frequency. As the frequency deviates further from the centralfrequency, the saturation effect weakens.WHY?Stimulated emission is most likely to occur at the central frequency. The incidentlight causes a rapid decrease in the number of inverted carriers.
